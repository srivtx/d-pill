# Proportion

How big things are, relative to each other. The ramp in `base.css` is the material. This is the relationship that makes the ramp look chosen. Optical alignment is `geometry.md`.

## Ratios that read as a decision

Neighboring steps on the ramp look like indecision. Skip at least one step between a parent and what it contains.

- A poster headline is at least 2.5 times the body, and it may be 4 times. Under 2 times, it is a heading, not a poster.
- A tool title is `--text-xl` against `--text-md`. The meta under it is `--text-sm`. That is enough. Do not also make the title bold, colored, and underlined.
- A price is the largest number on a selling page. The compare-at price is clearly smaller and quiet.
- A margin is larger than a gutter. A section gap is at least twice the gap inside a group. If those are close, the page is one lump.

The golden ratio (about 1.62) is a check, not a layout engine. If two blocks are almost the same width and should not be, pushing them toward 3:2 or 2:1 will look more decided than 6:5. Do not set every margin to phi. Do not overlay a spiral.

## The first screen

One mass owns about half of the first viewport. The headline, or the picture, or the list. Not three masses at a third each. The action sits with the mass it belongs to, and it is smaller than that mass (`sense.md`: the button is quieter than the sentence). On a tool, the list owns the screen and the title is a label for it.

## Down the page

Think in threes, as a rhythm: a large moment, a quiet moment, a large moment. Or the reverse. Two large moments in a row is shouting. Two quiet ones is a page that ran out.

Groups of peers are 2, 3, or 5. Two is a comparison. Three is a set. Five is a set you can still hold in your head. Four reads as a grid that is waiting for a fifth, unless it is a true catalog (the directory), where the count is the inventory and not a composition. Six equal feature cards is a template. Cut to three, or change the shape of the fourth.

## Width against height

A landscape picture feels like a place. A portrait feels like a person or a poster. A square feels like an object or a thumbnail. Choose from that, not from habit (`geometry.md`).

A very wide short band (a full-bleed strip of color with one line of type) is a poster device. Use it once. A tall narrow column is a label or a margin note. The main reading is never a tall narrow column under about 45ch if you can avoid it. Under 35ch the rag becomes a comb.

## Type and the measure

As the measure gets wider, leading gets looser (`type.md`). As the type gets larger, tracking gets tighter, a little, and only a little. A 72px word at 0 tracking looks loose. The same word at -0.04em looks set. A 14px word at -0.04em looks crushed.

Caps at display size may be tracked out (`styles.md`, film title, wayfinding). Caps at body size are `.caps`, small, or they are wrong.

## Space as pressure

Empty space is not leftover. On a poster, the empty field should feel like it is pushing the type into place. If you can move the type around freely and nothing gets worse, the space is slack. Tighten the relationship: align the type to the picture's edge, or to the margin, or to a third of the field. The third is a useful default for where a poster word sits. It is not a rule for dashboards.

## A check

Take the two most important elements. Estimate their size ratio. If it is under 1.5, they are competing. Make one of them clearly larger, or remove one.
