# Art

How a picture is made so it belongs to the page. Where the picture sits in the argument is `narrative.md`. Crops and bleed are `geometry.md`. This file is the picture itself.

## Have one, or have none

Do not draw a layout that needs a photograph and then invent one to fill the hole. Type and the ground are a finished page. A weak picture makes a strong page worse.

If you generate an image, constrain it to the direction: the page's hue, one light, no gradient backdrop, no floating interface, no fake logos, no people who are not the real subject. Then look at it next to the type. If it feels like a different website, do not use it.

## One light

Name the light before you shoot or generate. Morning from the left. A single soft window. A hard small source for a tool. Every photograph on the page uses that light. Mixed stock is a collage even when each frame is pretty.

Shadows keep color. A warm room has warm shadows. A gray shadow stamped under an object is a cutout. Catalog light (even, shadowless, the object floating on white) is for a shop grid where the object must match its neighbors. It is not for a poster. Quiet luxury and Atelier want darkness in the frame, real blacks, not a lifted gray fog.

Do not crush the grade into teal shadows and orange skin. One curve for the whole set. If you desaturate the set, they should still look like they were made together.

## What fills the frame

The object is large. Crop closer than the source. Hands doing the job beat a face looking at the camera. A face that stares out is a portrait, and it is the whole poster (Atelier), not a thumbnail beside a paragraph.

The subject looks toward the type or toward the work, not out of the page. Leave room in front of a gaze.

A product shot shows the decision, not the whole room. A wide shot is allowed once, as the establishing picture, and then you go close.

Backgrounds are the place, or they are nothing. A fake marble sweep, a floating podium, and a soft blob behind the product are three clichés. Seamless paper in the page's ground color is honest. A real table is honest. A generated "environment" that could sell anything is not.

## Type and the picture

Do not set type on top of a photograph. The picture is one mass. The type is the next mass. A caption sits under it, in the text face, `--text-sm`, a gap of `--space-3`, and it says the fact the picture cannot: the name, the place, the year.

If a headline must cross a picture, the picture has a quiet field big enough for the words, and the words are the one-color version of ink that clears 4.5:1. Test it. If it fails, move the type off the picture. A scrim (a fade to black) is a second material. Refuse it.

A screenshot of the product sits on `--bg` with a 1px `--line` if it is also light. No device frame, no tilt, no perspective, no shadow pretending the screen floats. The numbers in the shot match the numbers in the copy.

## Illustration

Only when the direction allows it: Play, and sometimes Soft. Flat shapes, the page's palette, one stroke weight. No gradients, no 3D clay, no characters the company does not have. If two illustrations cannot share a style, use none.

Icons are not illustrations. They are `icons.md`. Do not scale an icon up and call it the hero.

## Black and white

Black and white is a commitment for the whole set, not a treatment on one photo to make it "serious." It belongs to Editorial or Atelier when the color of the thing is not the point. UI chrome stays in the page's real colors. Do not make the interface grayscale to match the photos.

## Ratio

One ratio per region, repeated, from `geometry.md`. A shop grid may be square. A story is 3:2 or 4:5. Do not mix a panorama, a square, and a portrait in one section unless the mix is the one break on the page, and you can say why.
