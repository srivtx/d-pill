# Taste

The judgment under the rules. `sense.md` is how to see a poster. `craft.md` is the list of tells. This file is how you decide that something looks good, and what you do in the last pass when the page is merely correct.

Read it before you call a surface finished. The mark in `assets/mark.svg` is an iridescent capsule on purpose. That spectrum belongs to the object. It is not permission to paint the interface with it.

## It should look like it did not try

Beautiful work looks inevitable. The effort is in the proportion, and it is hidden. If you can see the effort (a gradient to prove skill, a shadow to prove depth, an animation to prove life), the page is trying. Take that thing off.

The test: would a careful person pause, or would they smell a template. Pausing comes from one clear shape and one surprise. The surprise is the one break in `sense.md`. A second surprise is showing off.

## One weather

Every surface on the page shares a temperature, a light, and an era.

Temperature is the hue of the ground (`color.md`). Light is one direction: the highlight, the photograph, and any shadow agree about where the light is. An era is a decade and a place. A 1970s poster, a glass dashboard, and a soft consumer app are three weathers. Pick the direction's weather and stay in it. Do not "modernize" one section.

Edge quality is part of the weather. Crisp type with a blurry, far shadow looks cheap, because the type is in one world and the shadow is in another. If the direction is flat, the edges are sharp and the shadow does not exist. If the direction is a photograph, the photograph has the only softness.

## Repeat the structure, vary one thing

Taste is repetition with a single change.

A list repeats the row. A magazine repeats the measure. What changes is one variable: scale, or crop, or the one accent. Changing scale and color and shape at once is noise. Changing nothing, not even the scale, is wallpaper. Wallpaper is the evenness in `sense.md`.

Across a whole site the nav, the type ramp, the radius, and the primary action stay put. The poster of each page may change shape. The furniture does not.

## The work is louder than the chrome

Cover the content with your hand. If the header, the cards, and the background are the interesting part, you designed the frame and neglected the picture. Quiet the frame. Chrome is smaller, muted, and still. The claim, the object, or the number is the loud thing.

A tool is allowed to have almost no picture. Then the loud thing is the row the person came to act on, and the button that does it. Not the sidebar.

## Typographic color

A paragraph is a texture. Squint at it. It should be an even gray cloth. Blotches mean a random bold word, a link styled like a button, or a line that is much shorter than the others in a way you did not choose.

Emphasis is one phrase. A paragraph with three bolds has no emphasis.

The headline's color is darker and larger, a stone on the cloth, not a hole in it. Do not set the headline in a light gray to be "subtle." Subtle is size and space. The headline stays ink.

## Numbers

Round marketing numbers look like a pitch: 10×, 99.9%, millions. A specific number looks like a person measured something: 40 bakeries, 18 fewer than last Tuesday, $48.

If you do not have the specific number, do not invent a round one. Leave the slot empty. A page with fewer claims looks more sure.

## Silence

Not every band needs an eyebrow, an icon, a paragraph, and a link. Most bands need one of those. A heading that is the sentence can stand with no kicker. A photograph can stand with no caption when the heading already said what it is. A caption that repeats the heading is noise. A caption that adds the fact the picture cannot hold is taste.

When you are unsure, remove the decoration and keep the structure. You can put one thing back. You rarely need to.

## Vibration and dirt

Full-saturation complements next to each other buzz. Red against green, pure blue against pure yellow, cyan against orange at full chroma. The eye cannot settle. That buzz reads as cheap even when the contrast ratio passes.

Dirty the chroma. The accent in `foundations.md` starts near 0.12, not 0.25. Large areas stay near the ground. The spectrum on the d-pill mark is small and on an object. Spread across a hero, the same spectrum becomes a costume.

## Finish

The last pass is petty, and it is the difference.

- Caps of icons and words share a line.
- Punctuation is real (“ ”, ’, …) and consistent.
- One date format, one time format, one currency format.
- The unit sits at the same height on every figure.
- Trailing spaces and double spaces are gone.
- The rag of the headline is a phrase, not an accident (`type.md`).
- Focus, hover, and press have been looked at, not only the default.
- The 390-wide poster still has a shape (`mobile.md`).

Then remove one treatment, as `craft.md` says. If the page feels more sure, that treatment was costume.

## What good looks like, by kind of page

- **A tool.** Quiet, dense, one accent, the row is the hero. You could use it for an hour.
- **A magazine.** Huge type, a rule, a lot of paper, one picture that is allowed to be large.
- **A shop.** The object and the price, light you believe, no badges begging.
- **A document.** A column you can read, links that look like links, no campaign in the margin.
- **A mark.** One object, recognizable at a glance, not a tiny picture of the product, not a button.

If the page could be any of these and you cannot tell which, you have not chosen.
