# Narrative

How a website argues, and how its pictures behave. The region-by-region structure is in `surfaces.md`. The words are in `writing.md`. Read this for a marketing page, a story page, or any page whose job is to be believed.

## The order of the argument

1. What this is, in the product's nouns, as the first screen.
2. Who it is for, if that is not obvious from the claim.
3. What changes for that person.
4. How it works, shown more than described.
5. Proof you actually have.
6. What it costs, if you are selling.
7. How to start. Same destination as the first action.

You may cut a step. You may not open with a logo wall, a testimonial, or a pricing grid before the claim. The reader has not been given a reason to care yet.

One idea per section. The heading is the idea ("The dough waits overnight"), not a category ("Features", "Our process", "Why us"). If the heading could sit on a competitor's site, it is a category. Rewrite it until it can only be this product.

## Show the thing

A feature is the screen, the object, or the step, cropped to the decision. A metaphor ("supercharge your workflow") is not a feature. A paragraph about the metaphor is not a section.

How-it-works is three real steps with the product's verbs, or it is a single figure with captions. It is not six numbered circles.

## Proof

Proof is specific or it is absent.

- A number you can stand behind: "40 bakeries in the city." Not "thousands of teams worldwide."
- A customer mark you have permission to show. One color treatment, one height, aligned on the cap height, in a row. Not a marquee. Not a grayscale scrapbook of different sizes.
- One quotation, with a real name and a real role, set as a `blockquote` at 28ch. A wall of headshots is not more believable. It is louder.
- A before-and-after only if both sides are real and the same kind of picture.

If you have none of these, skip the proof section. Type and a clear price are more trustworthy than invented logos. `sense.md` and `craft.md` already forbid faking them. This is why: a reader who spots one fake mark discounts the rest of the page.

## The ask

The first screen has the action. The end of the page has the same action and the cost or the next step in one line. A long page may repeat it once in the middle. Three different offers ("Start free", "Book a demo", "Join the waitlist") in one page split the decision. Pick one primary destination. The others, if they exist, are text links.

Price is part of the argument when the page is selling. A pricing page that hides the number until a sales call is a different product. Do not design a fake "see pricing" maze for a number you have.

## Pictures

One light, one time of day, one ratio, across every photograph on the page. Mixed stock sources look like a collage even when each photo is fine.

People and animals look toward the type or toward the work, not out of the frame. Hands doing the job are usually better than a face looking at the camera. A portrait that stares at you is Atelier, and it is the whole poster, not a thumbnail beside a paragraph.

No photograph, no illustration: the page can be type, a rule, and the ground. That is a complete Editorial or Quiet luxury page. Do not generate a picture to fill a layout you drew before you had a picture.

Illustration, when the direction allows it (Play, sometimes Soft): flat shapes, the page's palette, one stroke weight, no gradients, no characters you invented that the company does not have. If the illustrations cannot share a style, use none.

A product screenshot is cropped to the decision, on `--bg`, with a 1px line if the screenshot is also light. No browser chrome, no tilt, no perspective, no floating shadow.

## Pace

Alternate the shape of sections, as in `sense.md`. After a dense section (a price, a list), the next section is quieter and more open. After a quiet section, you may bring a figure. Two dense sections in a row feel like a manual. Two empty sections in a row feel like the page ran out of thought.

The footer closes. The name, the real links, the year. It is not a second homepage.

## A doc or a story

Broadsheet and docs are not marketing. The claim is the title of the piece. The lede is one sentence that says what you will know after reading. Then the piece. No hero image behind the title. No newsletter modal. The contents list is the wayfinding. Pull quotes are rare, and they are sentences from the piece, not marketing lines you wrote beside it.
